# Lesson 06 - Tempo and Liquidation

Concepts
- A tempo is a unit of time in development.
- A move gains tempo when it develops one of your pieces while forcing the opponent to spend time answering a threat.
- An exchange in the opening is justified when it gains tempo or frees development.
- Liquidation means resolving central tension by exchange when that tension is holding up development or making the position awkward.
- Nimzowitsch warns against exchanging blindly. The exchange must have a concrete motive.

Strategic question
- If your opponent's queen or piece recaptures in the center, when is that good for you?

Strategic questions
- What useful thing does `Nc3` do even if the queen were not on `d5`?
- Why does Black have to spend time after `Nc3`?
- Why is the exchange on `d5` more purposeful than a random trade?

Explanation
- `Nc3` is a developing move.
- Because the queen is on `d5`, the same move also attacks the queen.
- Black must move the queen, so White has gained time in development.
- The exchange `exd5` enticed Black's queen onto an exposed central square.

Explanation
- In this example, Black liquidates with `...dxe4`.
- The goal is not an immediate attack on the queen.
- The goal is to reduce central tension and make development easier.
- Liquidation is therefore a practical cure when a tense center is slowing development.

Four Knights connection
- In Four Knights structures, both sides often develop knights early.
- A central exchange such as `d4`, `...d5`, `exd5`, or `...exd4` should be judged by whether it gains time, opens development, or relieves pressure.
- The lesson is not to memorize one line, but to ask whether the exchange improves the development race.

Exercise

Answer
- An exchange is good in the opening if it gains tempo or simplifies central tension so development can continue.
