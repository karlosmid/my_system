# Lesson 04 - Restraining a Mobile Center

Concepts
- When a free mobile center pawn has been allowed, it must be treated as dangerous.
- The opponent should either eliminate that pawn or restrain it until it loses its mobility.
- Restraint means placing enough pressure and control around the pawn that its advance becomes unattractive or impossible.
- In this lesson's example, Black's whole setup is aimed at White's `e4-e5` advance.

Strategic questions
- What is White trying to do with the pawn on `e4`?
- Which Black moves directly help keep `e4-e5` under observation?
- Why is `...d6` restraint, while a later `...d5` would be elimination?
- If White wants to make `e4-e5` work, which moves increase the pawn's mobility?

Explanation
- White's `e4` pawn is the mobile center pawn. Its advance to `e5` would gain space and attack the knight on `f6`.
- Black's `...d6` directly restrains `e5`, because the pawn on `d6` controls `e5`.
- Black's `...♞f6`, `...♝e7`, castling, `...♜e8`, `...♝f8`, and `...♝d7` complete mobilization while keeping White's center pawn under close watch.
- White's `f4`, `♖e1`, and similar moves try to support the advance `e4-e5`.
- Nimzowitsch's practical rule here is: if the center pawn cannot be taken cleanly, restrain it first; only later, when prepared, try to eliminate it.

Four Knights connection
- The same question appears in Four Knights structures after an early central exchange: if one side gives up a center pawn, the remaining enemy center pawn must not be allowed to advance freely.
- Do not call every exchange a surrender of the center. If the side without a center pawn has enough piece pressure and restraining moves, the center may still be under control.

Exercise
- [x] In the diagram position, identify White's main pawn advance and name two Black units that help restrain it.

Answer
- White wants `e4-e5`.
- Black's `♟d6` controls `e5`, and the rook on `e8` watches the `e`-file. The knight on `f6` also pressures `e4`, while Black's developed pieces support the restraint plan.
